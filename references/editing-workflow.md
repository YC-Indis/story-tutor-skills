# Editing workflow and gates

Use this reference to locate the current stage, prescribe the next pass, and prevent downstream polish from hiding upstream problems.

## 1. Start with an intention card

Before touching the timeline, define:

- intended viewer and viewing context;
- one-sentence story and angle;
- viewer promise;
- start and end state, physical and emotional;
- target duration;
- story-versus-retention position or section-level style curve;
- three aesthetic rules;
- deadline, current constraints, and definition of done.

Gate: the creator can explain why this film should exist and what a first-time viewer should experience.

## 2. File organization

Separate storage speed, working media, and backup/recovery responsibilities. Use a canonical project folder, naming convention, software template, reusable asset library, and a readable track hierarchy.

Prepare footage through Store → Organize → Import → Prepare. Verify media integrity, labels, sync, frame rate, color interpretation, audio, and proxy status. New files must land in the canonical folder before import.

Deliverable: `EDIT_READY` project package.

Gate: no unexplained offline media; footage can be restored and handed off without relying on memory.

## 3. Footage selection

Use two timelines or equivalent structures: a comprehensive selects space and the story cut. Execute:

1. add and inventory all footage;
2. synchronize before cutting;
3. watch everything, reject unusable material, separate viable material;
4. categorize by story-relevant attributes.

Evaluate A-roll for truth, clarity, performance, story function, and alternatives. Evaluate B-roll for action, context, emotion, continuity, transition, and sound. Keep an alternative when uncertainty is meaningful; do not hoard indistinguishable takes.

Deliverable: `SELECTS_LOCK`.

Gate: a useful shot or statement can be found quickly; every selected item has a reason; technical gaps and missing coverage are visible.

## 4. First assembly

Work macro to micro. Build the full story spine before perfecting scenes.

1. lay down only the essential A-roll, voice-over, or events;
2. test Selection, Chronology, and Duration;
3. add explicit placeholders for missing footage, narration, graphics, research, or sound;
4. round-trip the changed timeline back into the outline/script;
5. use temporary music only to test broad energy, not to hide weak structure.

Deliverable: `ASSEMBLY_V1` plus placeholder register.

Gate: the film plays from beginning to end; its task, conflict, progression, finale, and change can be understood without micro-polish.

## 5. Rough cut — scene creation

Build one scene at a time in this order:

1. **Story only:** define start state, end state, question, change, and essential A-roll/VO/events.
2. **Music hypothesis:** write a brief for emotional direction, energy, texture, and structural role before searching.
3. **Music surgery:** shape beginning, middle, and ending around the story; do not lengthen a scene for a favorite track.
4. **B-roll development:** assign every shot a story/context/emotion/reality/transition job.
5. **Remove pass:** delete repetition and anything whose information or feeling is already complete.

Distinguish a scene, which contains development in time/place, from a montage, which compresses or compares material around a purpose.

Deliverable: `ROUGH_SCENES_V1`.

Gate: each scene has a readable change; music and B-roll support rather than substitute for story; a scene exit ticket records unresolved risks.

## 6. Rough cut — scene transitions

Read the boundary before choosing a technique. Decide whether the next scene should feel connected or deliberately broken. Assess context, emotion, rhythm, movement, eye trace, time/space, and authenticity.

Start with a neutral cut. Then test visual methods such as opening/closing shots, fade, dissolve, match, smash, passing movement, whip, flare, or graphics only when justified. Test sound independently: fades, crossfades, audio matches, audio smash, sound bridge, or silence.

Use a transition intensity budget. Most boundaries should not compete for attention.

Deliverable: `ROUGH_CUT_V1` plus transition boundary map.

Gate: all scenes connect intentionally; the whole film survives a picture-only, audio-only, and normal playback.

## 7. Feedback

Write a feedback brief with intention, current stage, known constraints, and specific observation questions. Choose reviewers by representativeness and expertise, not quantity.

Possible methods:

- share the editing project with a trusted editor for technical/contextual inspection;
- watch live and record unprompted reactions;
- collect remote timecoded notes.

Do not explain the film while viewers watch. Capture confusion, attention loss, emotion, anticipation, recall, and interpretation. Separate problem evidence from the reviewer's preferred solution.

Evaluate each item by:

- alignment with the intended experience;
- recurrence and reviewer relevance;
- severity and affected dependency;
- supporting evidence;
- cost, reversibility, and acceptance test.

Deliverable: `FEEDBACK_CHANGE_LIST` with Accept / Test / Decline and rationale.

Gate: the change queue is ordered by dependency and impact; major story failures either return to rough cut or are consciously accepted.

## 8. Fine cut

Do not begin picture effects or final mixing. Execute four passes:

1. implement accepted feedback, largest dependencies first;
2. refine context, pacing, rhythm, continuity, eye trace, and transitions;
3. record final voice-over against the current cut;
4. finish required graphics/animation and remove every placeholder.

Use Selection, Chronology, and Duration as separate controls. Test early/neutral/late cut points rather than trimming randomly.

Deliverable: `FINE_CUT_LOCK` and a timing lock certificate.

Gate: full playback succeeds; sequence boundaries work; alternatives do not materially improve the film; placeholders are zero; further timing change has a named reason.

## 9. Visual polishing

After timing lock, triage shots by viewer-visible problem. Use three passes:

1. **Shot mechanics:** stabilization, crop/reframe, horizon, distortion, blur, or flip only with documented authenticity tradeoffs.
2. **Style and graphics:** digital movement, overlays, compositing, graphics, animation, and consistency.
3. **Subtitles:** accuracy, readability, line breaks, safe areas, timing, and intentional reveals.

Effects must solve a story, comprehension, continuity, attention, or aesthetic problem. Compare before/after with the effect toggled off.

Deliverable: `PICTURE_LOCK` plus shot polish log.

Gate: no remaining visual task would justify reopening timing; repeated treatments are consistent; fixes do not look less credible than the source.

## 10. Sound design

Lock picture before final sound. Work in this order:

1. clean and level A-roll/voice-over;
2. shape music around speech and story beats;
3. assess captured B-roll sound: keep, repair, replace, layer, or remove;
4. add ambience and diegetic details before non-diegetic flow/emotion effects;
5. adjust timing, level, frequency, space, and perspective;
6. perform micro then macro mix; test translation across playback systems.

Use an audio hierarchy: main voice → story-critical reality → emotional/flow support → optional texture. A convincing space usually needs reality before spectacle.

Deliverable: `AUDIO_LOCK`, dialogue/VO chain log, ducking map, and sequence sound map.

Gate: speech is intelligible; spatial reality is coherent; music and effects do not mask story; mix translates at different volumes and on ordinary devices.

## 11. Color

Separate color correction from creative grading. Ensure trustworthy monitoring and correct input color space/gamma.

For each scene:

1. choose a representative hero shot/frame;
2. correct exposure/brightness, white balance, and saturation;
3. write the psychological and aesthetic intention;
4. apply a restrained creative look or LUT;
5. match shots in a stable order;
6. apply selective masks, overlays, vignette, or noise reduction only where necessary.

Use waveform, RGB parade, histogram, and vectorscope according to their jobs; do not rely only on taste or a LUT.

Deliverable: `GRADED_MASTER_V1`, scene color brief, and match log.

Gate: shots match through playback, skin and important objects remain credible, highlight/shadow detail is controlled, and the look supports the story's emotional development.

## 12. Export, QC, and archive

Define delivery use before choosing settings. Preflight tracks, effects, in/out range, graphics, subtitles, audio, and color management.

Choose codec, resolution/aspect ratio, frame rate, bitrate, audio, and color tags from destination requirements. Use presets and unambiguous versioned filenames.

Review the rendered file, not only the timeline. Perform full playback and targeted stress checks for human omissions and render/software errors. Create alternative ratios, captions, stems, clean versions, or social cuts only for real use cases.

Deliverable: `DELIVERY_PACKAGE` with master(s), manifest, QC log, and recoverable archive.

Gate: render has been watched end to end; audio/video/color are correct on target devices; archive restores without personal memory.

## 13. Evidence book and next project

Record:

- intention and scope;
- stage versions and gates;
- major decisions with before/after evidence;
- feedback and resolutions;
- time spent by stage;
- quality and efficiency score;
- what failed and why;
- the next project's 1–3 improvement targets.

Match project level to attention budget:

- Beginner: complete story, reliable sound/image, export.
- Intermediate: story + engagement + one originality experiment.
- Advanced: story + engagement + flow, with a perfectionism budget.
